What is AWS Migration Hub?
Migration Hub is meant to be a service that gives you
a singular place for you to discover existing servers,
plan your migration efforts,
and track those migration statuses.
It's there to help you visualize your connections
and your server or database statuses that are a part
of your migration efforts.
It offers you options,
so you have an option to start your migration immediately
or you can group your servers into application groups first
and then start a migration later on.
Now it works by integrating with supported tools.
So it integrates with services
like the Application Migration Service
or the database migration service.
And you need to know
that it leverages other services, like we just talked about.
It only discovers and plans your migrations.
It has to work with the mentioned services
to actually do migrations themselves.
It can't do it by itself.
Now let's take a look at the migration phases.
The first is the discovery phase.
So this is where you're going to find your servers
and your databases so you can start to plan your migrations.
After all, you can't migrate if you don't know
what you have to migrate.
The second is to migrate.
So you connect tools to Migration Hub, like DMS,
which we just talked about, and you begin migrating all
of your infrastructure.
And then thirdly, the track phase.
So once your migration is underway,
you can track your statuses
and your progress of each individual portion
of the migration.
So with Migration Hub out of the way,
let's dive into this Server Migration Service.
AWS SMS is a service
that's meant to help you automate the migration
of your on-premise servers to the AWS Cloud.
And the beautiful thing about it is it's flexible.
It supports many VM types.
So we're talking things
like vSphere VMs, Hyper-V, and even Azure Virtual Machines.
It's very flexible
and covers a broad range of virtual machines,
and it works by using incremental replications
of your server VMs.
And what it does is it replicates your VMs
over to AWS AMIs that you can use to be deployed
on your Amazon EC2 instances.
So what are some use cases for this service?
Well, if you need to simplify migrations,
this helps you manage complex parts of a migration to AWS.
It can even handle processes like volume replication
so you don't have to do it by yourself.
It's also useful for multi-server migrations.
You can schedule replication intervals however you see fit.
You can then track the progress
for your chosen server groups as they're migrating.
And then, like we talked about, it's flexible.
So in addition to the many types of virtual machines,
it also supports many different operating systems.
Another use case is if you need to do incremental testing.
So since SMS does incremental migrations,
this allows you to perform quick testing
of your migrated servers.
So think small, iterative changes.
And lastly, say no to downtime.
So you can use SMS to actually minimize your down times
that would normally occur
during traditional migration efforts.
So if you need to migrate
to the cloud and avoid as much downtime as possible,
this may be the best service for you to use.
