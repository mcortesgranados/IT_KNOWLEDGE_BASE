So what is deregistration delay or connection draining?
Well, basically, it allows load balancers
to keep existing connections open if the EC2 instances
are deregistered or become unhealthy.
And this enables the load balancer
to complete in-flight requests made to the instances
that are deregistered or unhealthy.
So if you go in and deregister a EC2 instance,
deregistration delay will just keep that connection open
for people who are already using that EC2 instance.
Now, you can disable deregistration delay
if you want your load balancer
to immediately close the connections
to the instances that are deregistered
or have become unhealthy.
So let's have a look at how we can do this
in the AWS console.
Okay, so here I am in the EC2 portal
and then what I wanna do is just go down
to my load balancers.
We'll start with classic load balancers
'cause it's super easy.
So if you just click on your classic load balancer,
all you need to do is go over to your instances
and then in here, you can see connection draining.
This is the same thing as deregistration delay.
So you go in and hit edit,
and then you essentially just uncheck that box
and that will disable connection draining.
So that's how you do it for classic load balancers.
For application load balancers,
it's a little bit more tricky, but it's not too bad.
So if you just go over to your application load balancer,
what you actually need to do is go over
to your target groups over here
and then you edit the target groups.
You're doing this at the target group level,
not at the application load balancer level.
What you do is you just go over two attributes,
and in here, you can see target deregistration management.
So go ahead and hit edit
and then to disable it, you just change that to zero.
